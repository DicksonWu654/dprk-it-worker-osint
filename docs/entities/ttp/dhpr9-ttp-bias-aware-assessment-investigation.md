## Caveats

- Appearance, accent, nationality, ethnicity, language, geography, time zone, camera behavior, disability-related behavior, and location require corroboration before any adverse decision.
- Automated or platform-generated output is a review prompt. Consequential decisions require documented human review, correction, and appeal, rather than treating the output as a dispositive identity or misconduct determination.
- Technical-assessment review should use anonymized initial review where feasible, candidate explanation, multiple-reviewer validation, documented proportional action, and question-leak remediation.
- Nervousness, disability, communication style, camera refusal, accent, appearance, language, typing rhythm, geography, and unfamiliar tooling require corroboration before any deception finding. Session recordings and telemetry are sensitive and should be minimized, access-controlled, time-limited, and governed by law and policy. Consequential decisions require candidate feedback, correction, and appeal.
- Generic platform documentation is retained as defensive guidance; it supplies context rather than a DPRK incident, attribution, allegation, or measured effectiveness claim.

## Source-backed context

- For suspected technical-assessment or interview manipulation, define prohibited conduct in advance; gather multiple relevant facts; use more than one qualified reviewer where practical; separate an anonymized initial evidence review from identity disclosure when feasible; invite the candidate to explain; record the decision and rationale; and remediate leaked or compromised assessment content. One behavioral, camera, code, network, or location anomaly is insufficient on its own.
