## Source-backed context

- Treasury says that in 2025 North Korean IT workers in Russia sought ACH-enabled accounts at U.S. and other Western financial institutions using fraudulent U.S. identification documents so they could more readily receive direct-deposit payments from U.S. clients.
- Treasury assesses that U.S. digital-asset service providers face substantial risk of unknowingly employing DPRK IT workers and notes that IT-worker salaries are remitted in digital assets, particularly stablecoins.
- In summarizing the already-known $7.74 million civil-forfeiture matter, Treasury specifies laundering techniques including fictitious accounts, small structured transfers, chain hopping, token swapping, NFT purchases, U.S.-based online accounts, and commingling before proceeds were sent toward the DPRK.
- In summarizing the already-known four-worker digital-asset theft case, Treasury states that exchange accounts controlled by DPRK nationals but held in alias names were opened using fraudulent Malaysian identification documents.
