## Caveats

- Most detailed testimony in the 2024 OHCHR report concerns overseas labour generally, including non-IT sectors; conditions, deductions, controls, and percentages require source-specific support before applying them to an IT worker.
- This record retains no protected witness identifier or other interview-level personal data.
- Use this context only for system safeguards; demographic, nationality, language, geographic, socioeconomic, and student-status screening fall outside this use.

## Source-backed context

- OHCHR's 2024 thematic report describes a general overseas-labour model in which host employers pay DPRK-affiliated companies, workers receive a limited share after State and living-cost deductions, and compensation can remain subject to manager discretion.
- The same report describes surveillance and movement or information controls by Ministry of State Security officers or unit heads in overseas labour settings.
- A/80/149 separately places information-technology workers within the broader State overseas-labour system, but the detailed 2024 testimony concerns overseas labour generally and cannot be silently transferred to every IT worker.
