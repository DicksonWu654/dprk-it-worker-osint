## Source-backed context

- Kudelski describes recruitment of ordinary developers in Iran, Syria, and South Africa through LinkedIn followed by migration to WhatsApp, plus a structured ecosystem of assistants, HR screeners, callers, interviewees, subcontracted developers, local persons, and supporters. Local persons may provide identity/account/address support, handle corporate equipment, sign forms, or host laptops; callers may take interviews under another persona and manage communications or VPN services.
- Kudelski reports use of data brokers and editable document-image packages to support job-platform, social-media, interview, and background-check verification bypass, with persona switching between interviews and worker roles.
- Kudelski reports that suspected DPRK IT workers had developer-level access to the sanctioned hosting service Funnull and maintained or fixed its Goedge CDN-based delivery infrastructure, linking employment-fraud labor to a broader cybercrime-support ecosystem.
- Kudelski assessed automated TRX-to-USDT flows as supporting cash-out and infrastructure maintenance through a company-designated account.
