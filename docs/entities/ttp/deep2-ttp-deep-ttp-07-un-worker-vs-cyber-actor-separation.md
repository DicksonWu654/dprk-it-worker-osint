## Caveats

- A/HRC/61/55 adds UN provenance and an evidence boundary; its scope adds only that provenance and boundary.
- A/HRC/61/55's general overseas-worker labor language needs an express sector bridge before applying to DPRK IT workers.
- False-positive caution: A single cluster or person can participate in more than one track. Separation is the default analytic discipline; overlap remains an open question.
- False-positive caution: Bugs, vulnerable dependencies, and copied snippets are common. Intent requires stronger evidence.
- Japan MOFA file 100922716.pdf is a two-page media note; MSMT/2025/2 is a separate document.
- Later reporting documents some overlap; “distinct by default” remains a working rule rather than an absolute claim.
- National mirrors and official translations support provenance and accessibility; corroboration counts remain at the source-family level.
- Shared State management describes organizational structure; case-specific evidence is required before attributing intrusion, espionage, theft or other malicious activity to every revenue worker.
- The approximately ten-percent salary figure is a report-level estimate rather than a universal rule or transaction-level finding.

## Source-backed context

- A report-launch statement is not the full report it announces; cite MSMT/2025/2 itself for granular report claims.
- National hosts and official translations of one coordinated statement are one evidentiary publication family.
- General overseas-worker and forced-labor monitoring language does not establish an IT-worker-specific condition without an express sector bridge.
- UN Annex 29 states that IT workers and malicious cyber actors had separate bureaucratic organization, education, and skill sets; workers focused on revenue and were reportedly forbidden from malicious activity, although some accepted client hacking requests despite warnings. This requires analysts to distinguish employment fraud, insider abuse, and recruiter malware rather than collapsing them into one DPRK cyber label.
- A/80/149 refers separately to information-technology workers and cyberthreat operators while describing both as strategically educated, trained, dispatched, managed and controlled within a State labour system.
- The report describes self-funding pressure, assigned revenue quotas and communal work conditions, and reports that workers receive approximately ten percent of generated revenue as salary.
- Shared education and management do not erase the functional distinction: revenue-generating employment and malicious cyber operations should remain separate by default unless a source documents overlap in a specific case.
