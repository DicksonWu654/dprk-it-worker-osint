- Remote-work capability is a routine employment trait; DPRK affiliation is assessed from source-backed conduct and identity links.
- Claim attribution: Geoff White in Group-IB podcast; Mike Burgess in 60 Minutes Australia.
- Evidence class: cross-source qualitative synthesis.
- Fraudulent employment and fake-interview malware remain separate analytic tracks absent explicit same-case evidence.

- Accent, ethnicity, nationality, appearance, location, camera or audio quality, productivity level, consumer email, shipping change, meeting feature, and technical anomaly are contextual signals; fraud or DPRK attribution requires independent evidence.
- The sources provide qualitative observations; benign-work versus malicious post-hire prevalence is unmeasured.
- Use behavior and continuity signals only in combination with independent verification, consistently applied human review, least privilege, and an appeal or correction path.

## Source-backed context

- White says some workers may simply perform the assigned work and can be effective employees; Burgess separately says teams of multiple engineers can make a placement appear exceptionally productive. Both frame the core risk as concealed identity, sanctions exposure, and trusted access rather than poor job performance alone.
- Do not use low productivity as the primary detection model; preserve identity and access controls for high-performing remote staff as well.
- Separate sanctions/identity risk from evidence of espionage, theft, extortion, or sabotage, and investigate each claim independently.
- Apply least privilege, access review, and post-hire identity continuity regardless of performance rating.
- Retain the source's observation class and do not generalize a controlled test, speaker claim, vendor sample, or demonstration into measured prevalence.
